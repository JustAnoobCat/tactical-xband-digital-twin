1. Project overview

        
The X-Band Digital Twin simulates an aerial communication node (ACN) serving sixteen registered ground nodes over a terrain-aware air-to-ground network. Ground nodes represent dismounted tactical terminals and a command-post class across six regional theaters and multi-biome terrains (Alpine Mountain, Arid Desert, Open Plains / Urban, Tropical Jungle, and Maritime Littoral, alongside custom SRTM .hgt and uncompressed GeoTIFF DEM ingestion). Traffic is generated into QoS queues, links are evaluated from geometry and first-principles RF propagation models, and a TDMA scheduler allocates the shared uplink.

        
The platform supports offline flight-test CSV trace calibration (V3-05 2-parameter OLS empirical tuning and residual validation), telemetry inspection, 2D/3D tactical map views, scheduler diagnostics, and mission scenario authoring. It is intended for controlled research demonstrations, comparative scheduler evaluations, and digital-twin calibration studies; it does not connect to live airborne hardware-in-the-loop avionics, synchronize with real-time operational C2 networks, or establish field operational airworthiness.

        
Research comparison: Legacy_Static_TDMA assigns fixed equal uplink slots, while Tac-TAD adapts allocation using queue urgency, link state, and predictive terrain transitions. A fair comparison requires identical configuration, traffic, terrain, duration, and metric definitions.

4. UAV flight and trajectory model

        
Ellipse mode follows a rotated, arc-length-parameterized ellipse. Waypoint mode accepts a closed route (the last point connects to the first), validates the route, builds a cubic Hermite spline with shared bounded tangents, samples it at 512 samples per segment by default, and evaluates position by arc length. The route is therefore smooth and closed, but it is not a learned or aerodynamic flight controller.

        
    
          
  • Physical speed: the configured true-air-speed target in metres per second. The default is 60 m/s.
    • 
          
  • Speed transitions: positive acceleration and deceleration limits bound changes when speed or mode changes.
    • 
          
  • Stationary hold: a speed of 0 m/s freezes trajectory progress. This is a simulation hold and must not be interpreted as fixed-wing hovering.
    • 
          
  • Playback speed: a wall-clock frame-request multiplier (0.1×–10×; UI step 0.05×; presets 0.5×, 1×, 2×, 5×, 10×). It changes execution cadence only and does not alter the 20 ms physical timestep, the configured target airspeed, or link/scheduling results.
    • 
          
  • Telemetry: position and velocity provide heading through the velocity vector; altitude is configured as MSL.

6. RF propagation and link budget

        
The implemented link budget is an authoritative, first-principles deterministic physical model. Received power sums transmit power and directional antenna gains, then subtracts free-space path loss, terrain diffraction, gaseous absorption, rain loss, cable loss, and implementation margin. Thermal noise is coupled to physical sky-noise brightness temperature; SNR is received power minus effective noise in dB.

        
FSPL = 20 log₁₀(d₃D) + 20 log₁₀(f_c) + 20 log₁₀(4π/c)
P_noise = k_B T_sys B,    T_sys = T₀(10^(NF/10) − 1) + T_sky
SNR_dB = P_rx,dBm − (P_noise,dBm + ΔN_sky)

        
The airborne communication node (ACN) models a body-fixed 4-sector quadrant array (0°, 90°, 180°, 270° boresights: forward, starboard, aft, port) with ITU-R F.1336 parabolic azimuth/elevation roll-off (−12(Δφ/θ_3dB)² dB, θ_3dB = 65°), dynamic centripetal aircraft bank-angle coupling (roll angle computed from airspeed and yaw turn-rate), and 1.5 dB handover hysteresis at quadrant crossover boundaries to prevent sector chattering.

        
Slant-path atmospheric loss incorporates ITU-R P.676-13 two-component barometric scale-height gaseous absorption (integrating dry oxygen h_o = 6.0 km and water vapor h_w = 2.0 km exponential profiles). Precipitation attenuation uses ITU-R P.838-3 specific rain attenuation coupled with ITU-R P.839-4 0°C freezing isotherm partitioning (h_freeze = 4,800 m MSL in Ladakh), zeroing rain attenuation above the liquid-precipitation ceiling. Effective receiver noise couples directly to ITU-R P.618-14 sky-noise brightness temperature (ΔT_sky, ΔN_sky) driven by atmospheric and rain path absorption.

        
Topographic diffraction implements ITU-R P.526-15 §4.5.1 Bullington double-horizon equivalent knife-edge diffraction coupled with ITU-R P.526-15 §4.2 physical DEM crest radius of curvature R_crest cylinder attenuation T(m, n) computed from adjacent terrain profile samples. A discrete eight-tier DVB-S2X / 3GPP Rel-17 NTN adaptive coding and modulation (ACM) table maps instantaneous SNR to spectral efficiency and physical PHY bitrate. Obstacle intersections force outage classification. The platform features active, opt-in High-Fidelity and Multi-Biome physics engines: Zheng-Xiao isotropic/non-isotropic Rician-Jakes small-scale multipath fading, polarization mismatch loss and cross-polarization discrimination (XPD), two-ray ground Fresnel reflection, ITU-R P.833 vegetation canopy clutter, Goldhirsh dust storm scattering, dynamic electronic warfare (FHSS/DSSS ECCM, MVDR spatial beamforming, ESM direction-finding, and LPI/LPD power control), and multi-ACN / 2D MANET mesh relay routing.

      


      

        
7. TDMA scheduling algorithms

        
The simulation runs on a strict 20 ms (50 Hz) superframe consisting of a 1 ms control period, a dynamic 3–5 ms downlink broadcast window, and a 16 ms uplink window. Under the 4-Channel Parallel Per-Sector TDMA architecture, the ACN's four independent directional antenna quadrants (forward, starboard, aft, port) schedule transmissions concurrently without co-channel interference. Each quadrant operates an independent 16 ms uplink window, delivering 64 ms of aggregate concurrent spatial-reuse capacity per 20 ms frame (a 4× spatial multiplexing factor). Dynamic slots are constrained by a physical speed-of-light timing-advance guard (τ_guard = 12 μs) and a 200 μs minimum allocation quantum.

        
Dynamic resource allocation is governed by the Tac-TAD (Tactical Terrain-Aware Dynamic) scheduler, which operates in two coordinated passes:

        
    
          
  • Pass 1 (Urgency-Based Primary Allocation): Candidate nodes in each sector are ranked by urgency scores Ψ_i combining 5QI packet delay budgets, head-of-line delays, backlog, instantaneous SNR, exponential moving-average rates, and predictive terrain look-ahead occlusion pressure. Allocations are sized to meet demand while respecting channel capacity.
    • 
          
  • Pass 2 (Work-Conserving Deficit Drainage): If residual uplink airtime remains in any sector after primary allocations, Tac-TAD performs a second deficit drainage pass, reallocating uncommitted airtime to nodes with unsatisfied queued backlog in proportion to their remaining demand, guaranteeing work conservation across all four quadrants.
    • 
        

        
To preempt terrain-induced drop spikes, Tac-TAD integrates two critical mechanisms:

        
    
          
  • Effective Physical Deadline Compression: Impending terrain shadow truncates the effective time available to deliver queued traffic before line-of-sight is lost: τ_eff-TTL = min(τ_TTL, t_occ). This compresses packet deadlines against predicted occlusion time t_occ, boosting urgency to flush queues into usable channel capacity before obstruction.
    • 
          
  • Imminent-Expiry Protection Gate: When a candidate node's queue faces impending occlusion, look-ahead evacuation boost Φ_evac is conditionally gated: if any peer node has critical packets facing imminent drop (maxHolRatio ≥ 0.82, remaining TTL τ_TTL ≤ 30 ms, and τ_TTL < t_occ), Φ_evac is clamped to 1.0. This prevents look-ahead flushes from preempting packets that would otherwise expire immediately, resolving cross-node congestion regressions.
    • 
        

        
The digital twin features a 3-Way Lockstep Ablation architecture comparing three distinct scheduling policies under identical trajectory, traffic, and topographic conditions:

8. Queue, packet, and metric model

        
Each ground terminal maintains bounded strict-priority FQ-CoDel queues configured across four standard 3GPP 5QI tactical traffic classes:

        
    
          
  • 5QI 69 (Emergency C2): Strict tactical priority, 120-packet reserved buffer ceiling, 60 ms packet delay budget (PDB). Never dropped during shadow periods.
    • 
          
  • 5QI 65 (Mission-Critical PTT Voice): High priority, 75 ms PDB.
    • 
          
  • 5QI 67 (Mission-Critical Real-Time Video): Medium priority, 100 ms PDB, Poisson-distributed packet generation.
    • 
          
  • 5QI 70 (Tactical Reconnaissance Photo): Background priority, 300 ms PDB.
    • 
        

        
Total per-node buffer capacity is bounded at 400 packets. Lower-priority traffic is evicted first on buffer overflow; non-C2 packets are pruned when their sojourn delay exceeds their PDB. In Tac-TAD, Effective Physical Deadline Compression (τ_eff-TTL = min(τ_TTL, t_occ)) dynamically couples packet sojourn life to terrain obstruction look-ahead, triggering expedited transmission or managed evacuation before line-of-sight is severed.

        
Drop and throughput metrics are strictly classified and accounted across all four parallel quadrants:

        
    
          
  • Delivered Goodput: Payload bytes successfully dequeued and transmitted while the link is confirmed usable (SNR ≥ MODCOD threshold).
    • 
          
  • Outage Waste: Payload bytes dequeued by a scheduler arm into an unusable or severed link (prevalent in Legacy_Static_TDMA; eliminated in Reactive_M_LWDF and Tac-TAD).
    • 
          
  • Overflow Drops: Packets rejected at admission when queue buffer ceilings are saturated.
    • 
          
  • Delay / Expiry Drops: Packets discarded upon exceeding their packet delay budget.
    •

12. Research methodology and experimental evaluation

        
The intended evaluation is a paired, controlled comparison of Legacy_Static_TDMA and Tac-TAD. The two scheduler arms must receive the same initial terrain, ACN route and motion, node positions, traffic configuration, RF/environment parameters, simulation duration, and random inputs where randomness is introduced. Only the scheduler policy should differ. A single browser session is useful for exploration, but comparative claims should be generated from recorded run manifests and repeatable worker runs.

12.3 Statistical evaluation plan

        
For each paired scenario, calculate the Tac-TAD minus Static difference (and the ratio only when the denominator is meaningful). Summarize paired differences with mean, median, standard deviation, interquartile range, and confidence intervals. Use a paired t-test only when the distribution and independence assumptions are defensible; otherwise use a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test or a permutation/bootstrap interval over scenario-level paired differences. Report effect sizes alongside p-values, correct for multiple primary comparisons when several hypotheses are tested, and treat confidence intervals crossing zero as inconclusive rather than as evidence of equivalence.

        
Frames within one run are temporally correlated, so they must not be treated as independent experimental replicates. The recommended unit of inference is the paired scenario/run, with seeds or independently constructed scenarios as replicates. Pre-register primary metrics, exclusion rules, minimum run counts, and stopping criteria before inspecting comparative outcomes.

Notation and units. Distances are in metres, time in seconds, frequency in hertz, speed in metres per second, power in dBm or watts as stated, and angles in radians unless degrees are explicitly shown. Every equation below has a unique appendix identifier and a source/function mapping.


        
A.1 Radio propagation and link geometry

        
(A-1)   d₂D = √(Δx² + Δy²),    d₃D = √(Δx² + Δy² + Δz²)
         Δx = xA − xN,   Δy = yA − yN,   Δz = zA − zN

        
Variables: (xA,yA,zA) is the ACN position in m MSL/local Cartesian coordinates; (xN,yN,zN) is the node position; Δx,Δy,Δz are component differences; d2D is horizontal range and d3D is slant range, all in m. Interpretation: this is the separation used by propagation delay and FSPL. Source: established Euclidean geometry; TerrainEngine.computeSlantGeometry in src/core/terrain.ts. Limits: local Cartesian coordinates and endpoint heights are assumed; geodetic transformation and terrain datum errors are outside this equation.

        
(A-2)   τprop = d₃D / c,    τTA = 2τprop = 2d₃D / c

        
Variables: τprop is one-way propagation delay in s; τTA is the physical two-way timing advance in s; c is the configured speed of light in m/s. Interpretation: Both LinkBudgetEngine and the parallel per-sector TDMA schedulers compute and record the exact physical speed-of-light two-way propagation timing advance τ_TA = 2·d_3D / c (in seconds and microseconds) on every SlotAllocation and node telemetry record. Source: established physical propagation identity; TerrainEngine.computeSlantGeometry, LinkBudgetEngine.evaluateLink, and parallel per-sector slot allocation timing advance calculations in all scheduler implementations. Limits: clock drift, network synchronization offsets, and hardware waveform processing delays are not modeled.

        
(A-3)   φ = d₂D / R′e,    e = atan2((R′e + zA)cosφ − (R′e + zN), (R′e + zA)sinφ)

        
Variables: R'e = kRe is effective Earth radius in m; φ is central angle in rad; e is elevation angle, converted to degrees for telemetry. Interpretation: the implementation uses an effective-radius Earth geometry. Source: standard-based approximation associated with ITU-R P.834; computeSlantGeometry and EnvironmentConfig.effectiveEarthRadiusM. Limits: k = 4/3 is fixed by default; no measured refractivity profile is used.


        
A.2 Terrain obstruction, LOS/NLOS and Fresnel-zone analysis

        
(A-4)   s = d₁ / D,   (x(s),y(s),zLOS(s)) = (xN,yN,zN) + s(Δx,Δy,Δz)

        
Variables: D = d3D; d1 is distance from node to a sampled path point; d2 = D − d1; s is the path fraction; zLOS is linearly interpolated endpoint height. Interpretation: the terrain engine samples this ray at a configured interval, normally 100 m. Source: project-defined path sampling in TerrainEngine.evaluateTerrainObstruction. Limits: finite sampling can miss sub-interval terrain features; out-of-coverage paths return invalid clearance rather than fabricated terrain.

        
(A-5)   hclear(d₁) = zLOS(d₁) − [hDEM(d₁) + bEarth(d₁,d₂)]
         bEarth(d₁,d₂) = d₁d₂ / (2R′e)

        
Variables: hDEM is sampled terrain elevation in m; bEarth is effective-Earth bulge in m; hclear is clearance in m. Interpretation: positive clearance is above the effective terrain/curvature surface. Source: project implementation in TerrainEngine.computeEarthBulge and evaluateTerrainObstruction, with effective-radius basis from ITU-R P.834. Limits: the parabolic bulge is an approximation and is not a full spherical ray-trace or refractivity solution.

        
(A-6)   r₁(d₁,d₂) = √(λd₁d₂ / D),    ρ = hclear / r₁

        
Variables: r1 is first Fresnel-zone radius in m; λ = c/fc is wavelength in m; ρ is normalized clearance. Interpretation: the engine retains the path sample with minimum normalized Fresnel clearance. Source: established Fresnel-zone relation; evaluateTerrainObstruction. Limits: only the first-zone scalar clearance is used; antenna patterns, phase, multipath, and partial-zone field integration are not modeled.

        
(A-7)   LOS = (ρ ≥ 0.6) ∨ (0 < ρ < 0.6) with diffraction penalty; NLOS/outage geometry when ρ ≤ 0

        
Variables: ρ is the minimum ratio in (A-6). Interpretation: the implementation preserves an isLos boolean for positive clearance, but assigns a nonzero diffraction loss when clearance is below 0.6 Fresnel radius; nonpositive clearance is treated as terrain obstruction. Source: project-defined threshold behavior in evaluateTerrainObstruction and linkStatusClassifier.ts. Limits: the threshold is not presented as a universal LOS standard.


        
A.3 Multi-Ridge Diffraction: ITU-R P.526-15 Bullington & Spherical Crest Curvature

        
(A-8)   θTx = maxi[(hi − zTx)/d1,i],    θRx = maxi[(hi − zRx)/d2,i]
         db = [zRx − zTx + θRxD] / [θTx + θRx],    hb = zTx + θTxdb
         νb = hb √[(2/λ)(1/db + 1/(D − db))]

        
Variables: θTx and θRx are horizon elevation angles from Tx and Rx; db and hb are distance and height of the equivalent Bullington knife-edge; νb is the dimensionless Bullington diffraction parameter. Source: ITU-R P.526-15 §4.5.1 Bullington double-horizon method implemented in TerrainEngine.evaluateTerrainObstruction.

        
(A-9)   LBullington = 6.9 + 20log₁₀(√[(νb − 0.1)² + 1] + νb − 0.1)
         Rcrest = Δs² / |hi−1 − 2hi + hi+1|
         m = Rcrest [π/(λ R′e²)]1/3,    n = hclear [π²/(λ² R′e)]1/3
         T(m,n) = 7.2 m1/2 [1 − exp(−0.25 n)],    Ldiff = LBullington + T(m,n)

        
Variables: LBullington is knife-edge loss in dB; Rcrest is local terrain radius of curvature computed from adjacent DEM samples in m; T(m,n) is the ITU-R P.526-15 §4.2 cylinder curvature penalty replacing legacy heuristic constants; Ldiff is total diffraction loss in dB. Source: TerrainEngine.evaluateTerrainObstruction.


        
A.4 Antenna Arrays, Atmospheric Attenuation, and Sky Noise Coupling

        
(A-10)   Gsector(θ) = G0 − min[12(θ/θ3dB)², Gsidelobe]
         θaz,s = (ψLOS − ψheading − ψsector,s) mod 360°
         Handover Hysteresis = 1.5 dB (prevents chattering at ±45° crossover boundaries)

        
Variables: Gsector is ITU-R F.1336 parabolic roll-off gain in dBi; θ3dB = 65°; Gsidelobe = 25 dB; ψsector,s ∈ {0°, 90°, 180°, 270°} for forward, starboard, aft, and port body-fixed quadrants. Source: QuadSectorAntennaEngine in src/core/antennaArray.ts.

        
(A-11)   FSPL = 20log₁₀(d₃D) + 20log₁₀(fc) + 20log₁₀(4π/c)
         Lgas = ∫₀ᴰ γgas(z(s)) ds,    γgas(z) = γ0 exp(−z / Hscale)
         Lrain = γR min(d₃D/1000, max(0, (hfreeze − znode)/sin(e)))

        
Variables: Lgas integrates ITU-R P.676-13 barometric scale-height absorption (H_scale = 2.0 km for H₂O, 8.0 km for dry air); Lrain implements ITU-R P.838-3 specific rain attenuation capped at ITU-R P.839-4 0°C isotherm freezing ceiling (h_freeze = 4,800 m MSL in Ladakh). Source: LinkBudgetEngine.evaluateLink.

        
(A-12)   Tsky = Tcosmic 10−Aatm/10 + Tatm(1 − 10−Aatm/10),    Tatm = 275 K, Tcosmic = 2.7 K
         Tsys = T₀(10NF/10 − 1) + Tsky,    ΔNsky = 10log₁₀(1 + Tsky / [T₀(10NF/10 − 1)])
         SNRdB = Prx,dBm − (Pnoise,dBm + ΔNsky)

        
Variables: Tsky is antenna noise temperature from ITU-R P.618-14 atmospheric brightness coupling; ΔNsky is the resulting sky-noise degradation factor in dB added to receiver thermal noise. Source: LinkBudgetEngine.evaluateLink.


        
A.5 Receiver noise, SNR and link adaptation

        
(A-13)   Pnoise,W = kBT B 10NF/10,    Pnoise,dBm = 10log₁₀(1000Pnoise,W)
         SNRdB = Prx,dBm − Pnoise,dBm

        
Variables: kB is Boltzmann’s constant in J/K; T is temperature in K; B is bandwidth in Hz; NF is noise figure in dB; powers are W/dBm. Source: established thermal-noise relation and dBm conversion; computeThermalNoiseFloorDbm and evaluateLink. Limits: constant temperature and scalar noise figure are assumed; interference and implementation-specific receiver effects are represented only by configured margin.

        
(A-14)   η(SNR) = ηj for the highest threshold γj ≤ SNR;    RPHY = η(SNR)B

        
Variables: γj is a MODCOD minimum-SNR threshold in dB; ηj is spectral efficiency in bit/s/Hz; η is selected efficiency; RPHY is nominal bitrate in bit/s. Source: threshold lookup and bitrate calculation in selectModCod and evaluateLink. Limits: this is link adaptation by table lookup, not a waveform, coding, HARQ, or modem simulation.


        
A.6 MODCOD and PHY capacity

        
The current table contains eight named tiers from pi/2-BPSK 1/3 at −1.5 dB and 0.33 bit/s/Hz through 64-APSK 5/6 at 22 dB and 4.95 bit/s/Hz. If no threshold is met, the table returns LINK_OUTAGE, zero spectral efficiency, and zero bitrate. These names are DVB-S2X / 3GPP Rel-17 NTN-inspired labels, not a claim of standards-compliant PHY behavior.

        
(A-15)   Cslot = ⌊τslotRPHY⌋ bits,    Bslot = ⌊Cslot/8⌋ bytes

        
Variables: τslot is scheduled payload duration in s; Cslot is integer slot capacity in bits; Bslot is dequeuing capacity in bytes. Source: LegacyStaticScheduler.scheduleFrame and TacTadScheduler.scheduleFrame. Limits: PHY capacity is an upper bound for the slot; actual delivered application goodput depends on queued bytes, packet fit, outage state, and scheduler service.


        
A.7 TDMA framing and resource allocation

        
(A-16)   TUL,avail = Tframe − Tctrl − TDL
         Tframe=0.020 s, Tctrl=0.001 s, TDL∈{0.003,0.005} s

        
Variables: all terms are seconds; TUL,avail is available uplink time. Source: constants and scheduler arithmetic in src/core/scheduler.ts. Interpretation: the current rule selects 3 ms normally or 5 ms when downlink backlog exceeds 5,000 bytes; a 7 ms maximum constant exists but is not reached by the current rule. Limits: this is not a complete bidirectional MAC protocol.

        
(A-17)   τstatic = [TUL,nominal − Nτguard] / N

        
Variables: N is registered node count; τguard = 12 μs; TUL,nominal = 16 ms; τstatic is equal payload slot duration. Source: LegacyStaticScheduler.scheduleFrame. Limits: static service dequeues packets even when the link is unusable, which is intentionally measured as outage waste in this model.

        
(A-18)   Tused = Σi∈alloc(τi + τguard),    UUL = 100 Tused/TUL,avail

        
Variables: τi is allocation duration; Tused is scheduled airtime including guards; UUL is utilization in percent. Source: both scheduler reports. Limits: utilization is airtime accounting, not application throughput or energy efficiency.


        
A.8 Traffic generation and queueing theory

        
(A-19)   λv = μvideo,frame,    K ~ Poisson(λv)

        
Variables: λv is configured mean video packets per frame; K is generated video packet count. Source: GroundNodeTrafficSubsystem.poissonDraw and generateTraffic. Limits: C2, voice, and photo generation follow deterministic interval/window/remaining-bytes rules; the Poisson process is per frame and is not a calibrated field traffic model.

        
(A-20)   Wp(t) = max(0,t − tarrival,p),    tdeadline,p = tarrival,p + PDBq

        
Variables: Wp is packet sojourn time in s; tarrival and tdeadline are packet timestamps; PDBq is the QoS profile delay budget. Source: Packet, enqueuePacket, getHolDelayS, and queue pruning in src/core/queues.ts. Limits: deadline pruning excludes 5QI 69 during the modeled shadow behavior; this is a project policy, not a universal QoS rule.

        
(A-21)   Qbits(t+) = Qbits(t) + Abits(t) − Dbits(t) − Xoverflow,bits(t) − Xexpiry,bits(t)

        
Variables: all terms are bits; Qbits is queued backlog; Abits is admitted packet payload; Dbits is dequeued service; Xoverflow,bits and Xexpiry,bits are the sizes of evicted or expired packets. Separately, runtime counters droppedOverflowCount and droppedDelayCount count packets, not bits. Source: project-defined conservation summary of FQCoDelQueue.enqueue, dequeue, and pruneExpiredPackets. Limits: the compact equation hides per-class ordering, packet-size fit, buffer ceilings, and eviction priority.


        
A.9 3-Way MAC Ablation: Static TDMA, Reactive M-LWDF, and Tac-TAD

        
(A-22)   Ψi = Φevac,i · Ui · (Ri/max(1000,R̄i)) · max[1, log₂(1 + Qi/8000)] · Ptactical,i
         Ui = Σq∈active wq exp(min(8, HOLi,q/PDBq))
         Ablation Policy: Reactive M-LWDF enforces Φevac,i ≡ 1.0 (lookahead disabled)

        
Variables: Ψi is dynamic scheduling urgency score; Φevac is lookahead evacuation multiplier; Ui is QoS urgency; wq is class weight; HOL is head-of-line age; PDB is packet delay budget; Ri is instantaneous bitrate; R̄i is exponential moving average rate; Qi is backlog bits; Ptactical,i is node tactical priority. Source: TerrainAwareDynamicScheduler and ReactiveMlwdfScheduler in src/core/scheduler.ts.

        
(A-23)   τreq,i = Qi/Ri,    τdrain,i = Qi/Ri,    ζi = τdrain,i / max(tocc,i, 0.020 s)
         τeff-TTL,i = min(τTTL,i, tocc,i),    effHolRatioi = max[maxHolRatioi, clamp0..0.95(1 − 0.5 tocc,i / max(τTTL,i, 0.060))]

        
Variables: τdrain is required queue drain time in s; ζi is dimensionless queue-drain evacuation pressure; τeff-TTL is effective physical deadline compressing 5QI TTL against impending terrain shadow; effHolRatio scales deadline urgency before link loss. Source: rankCandidateNodes in src/core/scheduler.ts.

        
(A-24)   Critical Peer Protection: if peer j has maxHolRatioj ≥ 0.82 ∧ τTTL,j ≤ 30 ms ∧ τTTL,j < tocc,i, then Φevac,i = 1.0
         Otherwise: Φevac,i = 1 + (Φraw,i − 1) · clamp0.15..1.5(ζi + 0.25 / max(tocc,i, 0.05)) · [1 + 0.5 (1.5 − tocc,i)/1.5]

        
Variables: tocc is predicted time to occlusion in s; the imminent-expiry protection gate ensures pre-occlusion flushes yield when a peer's packet is in imminent drop danger (≤ 30 ms remaining TTL), completely preventing congestion preemption regressions. Source: TerrainAwareDynamicScheduler.scheduleFrame.


        
A.10 Simulation timestep, state evolution and telemetry

        
(A-25)   tn+1 = tn + Δt,    fn+1 = fn + 1,    Δt = 0.020 s = 1/50 s

        
Variables: tn is simulated time; fn is frame index; Δt is the fixed physical timestep. Source: SimulationWorkerManager.tickSimulation and FRAME_DT. Interpretation: each tick refreshes obstruction state, generates/prunes traffic, runs both schedulers, accumulates metrics, and emits a telemetry frame. Limits: wall-clock timer interval and playback speed affect execution cadence only; they do not change Δt.

        
(A-26)   yn = {tn, fn, pACN, vACN, Li, Qi, Si, Mn}

        
Variables: yn is the published telemetry observation; pACN/vACN are position/velocity; Li is link state; Qi is queue state; Si is schedule/report state; Mn is accumulated metrics. Source: buildTelemetryFrame. Limits: telemetry is a snapshot at a worker frame boundary; it is not an event-sourced log of every internal operation.


        
A.11 Performance metrics and statistical evaluation

        
(A-27)   G = Σ bitsdelivered / Teval,    Woutage = Σ bitsdequeued,outage
         Bfinal = bitsbacklog at final frame,    Ddrop = Ddelay + Doverflow

        
Variables: G is delivered goodput in bit/s; Teval is the stated evaluation interval; Woutage is outage-wasted payload bits; Bfinal is the final-frame backlog; Ddrop separates delay and overflow packet counts. Source: worker metric accumulation and FrameScheduleReport; staticFinalBacklogBits and tacFinalBacklogBits are overwritten each frame rather than accumulated. Limits: denominators and window boundaries must be reported; PHY capacity, scheduled service, dequeued payload, and delivered goodput are different quantities.

        
(A-28)   Δj = mTac,j − mStatic,j,    CIα(Δ̄) = Δ̄ ± t1−α/2,n−1sΔ/√n

        
Variables: Δj is a paired scenario difference; m is a metric; n is the number of independent paired scenario/run replicates; sΔ is the sample standard deviation; CIα is a confidence interval when the paired-t assumptions are reasonable. Source: project evaluation formulation described in the methodology section, not runtime code. Limits: use paired Wilcoxon or bootstrap/permutation alternatives when assumptions fail; do not treat correlated frames as independent replicates.

15. Limitations and future research

        
Implemented capabilities

        
    
          
  • Browser TypeScript worker simulation at a 20 ms physical step with strict Web Worker state authority.
    • 
          
  • 4-sector ITU-R F.1336 antenna array with bank-angle coupling, ITU-R P.526-15 Bullington + crest curvature diffraction, P.676-13 gas absorption, P.838/839 rain loss, P.618-14 sky noise, 4-channel parallel TDMA with deficit drainage, 3-way ablation (Static, Reactive M-LWDF, Tac-TAD), trajectory editing, obstacle geometry, map views, and telemetry history.
    • 
          
  • Opt-in High-Fidelity & Multi-Biome RF engines: Zheng-Xiao Rician-Jakes small-scale fading, polarization mismatch / XPD, two-ray Fresnel ground reflection, ITU-R P.833 foliage canopy loss, Goldhirsh dust storm attenuation, electronic warfare (FHSS/DSSS ECCM, MVDR beamforming, ESM/LPI/LPD, GNSS spoofing defilade), multi-ACN 2D MANET routing, and offline flight-test trace calibration (V3-05 2-parameter OLS).
    • 
        

        
Simplifications and operational boundaries

        
    
          
  • The simulation does not interface with live airborne hardware-in-the-loop avionics or operational C2 tactical datalinks in flight.
    • 
          
  • High-fidelity physics (multipath, polarization mismatch, dust, foliage, EW jamming) operate as configurable, calibrated discrete-event models rather than full-wave 3D electromagnetic Maxwell solvers.
    • 
          
  • The MODCOD table is an 8-tier engineering abstraction and is not proof of full-standard DVB-S2X or 3GPP Rel-17 conformance under arbitrary hardware impairments.
    • 
          
  • History stores rolling telemetry snapshots for diagnostic inspection and does not provide deterministic re-execution from an immutable event log.
    • 
          
  • Legacy Python artifacts and archived benchmark numbers are historical reference baselines; active production benchmarks derive from the TypeScript engine.
    •
